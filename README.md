# On the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-Functions

Robert Martinez (REM2) · Preprint · November 26, 2026

[Download the full paper (PDF, 655 pages)](GRHDLF.pdf)

This repository contains On the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-Functions, a complete proof in prose of the Riemann hypothesis and of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for all Dirichlet L-functions. Part I shows, by classical means, that the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to an inequality for a smoothed prime-power sum at the fourth powers of integers. It then proves that a one-sided bound for a quadratically twisted form of that sum, in an arithmetic progression, implies the Riemann hypothesis and the generalized Riemann hypothesis for every Dirichlet character, by an oscillation argument for Mellin transforms. Part II proves the twisted bound in full: a nonzero lattice vector built from a chain of theta lifts is divisible by many prime powers, and comparing its length with a height bound controls the twisted sum. The proof of the twisted bound follows the Lean code released by Levent Alpöge (20 November 2026) and by Luke Sernau et al. (25 November 2026), employees of Anthropic, in collaboration with an internal, unreleased model of Claude. The manuscript gives the argument as a complete proof in prose, citing only the published literature.
